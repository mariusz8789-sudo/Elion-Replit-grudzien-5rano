# Genesis Scientific Pilot: Initial Laboratory Shortlist

## Purpose

This shortlist identifies plausible first external pilot partners for Genesis, with emphasis on a narrow and defensible pilot: **source-backed natural-product candidates, a declared neurobiological target, and an independent in-vitro validation request**. The shortlist does not claim that any institution has agreed to collaborate, nor that the listed facilities can execute the exact proposed assay without a technical and commercial discussion.

## Recommended first contact

The strongest first fit is the **Laboratory of Neuropharmacology and Epigenetics at the Maj Institute of Pharmacology, Polish Academy of Sciences (IF PAN), Kraków**, led by Professor Małgorzata Kajta. Its published profile explicitly covers neuroprotective substances, neuronal and microglial models, human iPSC-derived neurons, stroke and Alzheimer’s disease models, and molecular/cellular readouts including viability, cytotoxicity, oxidative stress, apoptosis, autophagy, qPCR, ELISA, western blotting and confocal microscopy.[1] The laboratory also publishes work involving natural or phytochemical compounds such as amorfrutin B and DIM, which makes it materially closer to Genesis’s natural-functional-discovery goal than a generic computational or neuroscience group.[1]

A first pilot discussion should therefore ask whether the laboratory would accept a small, pre-registered, research-only study in which Genesis supplies a provenance-preserving candidate dossier and the laboratory independently decides the assay design, controls, concentrations, and acceptance criteria. Genesis should not prescribe dosing, claim efficacy, or infer a mechanism from a binding record.

## Secondary candidates

The **Centre for Natural Products Discovery at Liverpool John Moores University** is the strongest complementary partner for natural-product chemistry, analysis, pharmacology and toxicology. Its public description lists phytochemistry, natural-products pharmacology, toxicology, HPLC, NMR, LC-MS, HR-MS, X-ray crystallography, and in-vitro/ex-vivo bioassay and cell-biology facilities.[2] It is a good candidate for compound identity, purity, analytical confirmation and pharmacology support, although the public page does not establish a specific A1-receptor assay or a commitment to a Genesis pilot.

The **Laboratory of Neurobiology at the Nencki Institute, Warsaw**, led by Professor Leszek Kaczmarek, is a strong neuroscience validation candidate for synaptic plasticity and neuropsychiatric biology. Its public profile focuses on molecular and cellular mechanisms of learning, memory, synaptic plasticity and MMP-9-related neurobiology, with a substantial record of experimental neuroscience and translational collaborations.[3] It is less directly aligned with natural-product pharmacology or A1 receptor binding than IF PAN, but could be valuable for a later mechanism or phenotype study if a candidate survives the initial target-specific screen.

## Proposed pilot request

The most defensible first request is a **small feasibility consultation**, not a claim of drug efficacy. Genesis would provide one reference compound, a clearly labelled set of source-backed natural candidates, ChEMBL activity and assay context where available, PubChem identity and structure provenance where available, RDKit properties, ADMET-AI model estimates, uncertainty, and a proposed validation request. The laboratory would independently confirm compound identity and purity, select an appropriate target-specific or functional assay, define controls, and specify the measurable endpoint.

For the current A1-oriented example, the biological claim should remain narrow: a ChEMBL binding record can support a research hypothesis about an A1 interaction, but it does not establish functional efficacy, neuroprotection, safety or clinical utility. ChEMBL publicly distinguishes target identity, target classification, activity types and assay counts; its A1 page lists the target as a single protein and shows separate activity categories including Ki, IC50 and EC50.[4]

The minimum handoff package should include the exact candidate identifiers, source URLs and retrieval dates, assay IDs, activity IDs, SMILES/InChIKey, all compute run IDs and versions, the deterministic artifact fingerprint, the validation request, and a statement of what is known, inferred and unknown. The laboratory should own the final experimental protocol and interpretation.

## Suggested outreach order

The practical order is **IF PAN first**, because its public research profile is closest to natural-product neuroprotection and offers directly relevant cell and molecular models. **LJMU second** is appropriate if the pilot needs analytical chemistry, natural-product characterization or broader pharmacology/toxicology capacity. **Nencki third** is appropriate for a later mechanistic or phenotype-validation phase rather than the first target-specific screen.

No outreach was sent in this step. Contacting a laboratory, sharing project material, or proposing a formal collaboration requires a separate decision about the sender, data package, confidentiality and external communication.

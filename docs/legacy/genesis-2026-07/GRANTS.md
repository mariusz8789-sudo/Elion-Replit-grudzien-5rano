# Grant Readiness

Honest assessment of readiness for public / non-dilutive funding. Genesis has a genuine
technical asset (a scientifically honest, provenance-first computational chemistry platform)
but is **pre-traction** and **single-founder-scale**. Grants that reward *technical merit +
open science + honesty* fit better than grants that require a team, market traction, or a
clinical/biological result.

Cross-cutting strength for **all** programmes: the platform's scientific integrity —
fail-closed real computation, no fabricated values, explicit provenance, and a documented,
self-critical limitations register (KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md). Reviewers reward candour.
Cross-cutting weakness: **no external users, no team, no measured scientific validation of
outcomes** (the tool computes descriptors correctly, but has not been shown to improve a
real research decision).

---

## Horizon Europe (collaborative RIA/IA)
- **Strengths:** open, reproducible, transparent computational science fits the
  open-science mandate; clean architecture; strong test discipline.
- **Weaknesses:** Horizon wants **consortia** (multiple partners across countries) and
  societal-impact narratives. A solo codebase is not a consortium.
- **Missing:** academic + industry partners; a work-package plan; demonstrated impact.

## EIC Accelerator (deep-tech, company-stage)
- **Strengths:** clear deep-tech component; a working product with billing already wired.
- **Weaknesses:** EIC expects a **company**, a team, market evidence, and TRL ~6+. Genesis
  is pre-company, pre-traction; the honest TRL of the *commercial* product is ~4–5.
- **Missing:** incorporated entity, team, letters of intent / pilot customers, a credible
  scale-up and IP story.

## National R&D grants (e.g. NCBiR / national innovation agencies)
- **Strengths:** often the **best near-term fit** — smaller, national, tolerate early-stage
  single applicants or a company + one research partner; reward a working prototype.
- **Weaknesses:** usually require a registered entity and co-financing; some expect an
  industrial partner.
- **Missing:** legal entity, one research/industry partner, a costed work plan.

## University collaborations
- **Strengths:** the honest-provenance framing is genuinely attractive to academic
  med-chem/cheminformatics groups; low barrier — offer the tool for a real campaign.
- **Weaknesses:** collaborations need a champion and a concrete shared question.
- **Missing:** a named academic partner and a pilot dataset. **This is the recommended first
  move** — it also produces the validation evidence every other programme wants.

## Research institutes / CROs
- **Strengths:** provenance + reproducibility + export map directly to institute/CRO
  documentation needs.
- **Weaknesses:** they need reliability, support, and data security that the current
  single-process, plaintext-token, no-backup backend does not yet provide.
- **Missing:** the production-hardening in KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md §2; a support commitment.

---

## Recommended sequence
1. **One university pilot** (cheapest, produces validation evidence + a co-applicant).
2. **National R&D grant** with that partner (best structural fit).
3. Horizon/EIC only **after** a team, an entity, and pilot evidence exist.

Do not overstate readiness in any application: Genesis is a credible **early-stage
computational tool with exceptional scientific honesty**, not a validated discovery platform.
